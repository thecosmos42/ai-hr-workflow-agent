# New-Hire Onboarding Policy Handbook

Document owner: People & Culture. Applies to every hire starting on or after the effective date of this edition. Where this handbook and a hiring manager's verbal instruction differ, this handbook applies.

## §1 Purpose & scope

This handbook defines how new employees are onboarded: who does what, which equipment and system access a new hire receives, which trainings are mandatory, and when a case must be handed to a human for decision.

### What this handbook covers
- Permanent, fixed-term and contractor hires starting in the Netherlands.
- Employees based at the Eindhoven office, the Amsterdam office, or working remotely from the Netherlands.
- Equipment ordering, system access provisioning, mandatory trainings, right-to-work checks and the day-1 schedule.

### What this handbook does not cover
- Offboarding and internal transfers.
- Recruitment and offer negotiation.
- Payroll set-up, which is handled by Finance under a separate procedure.

### Guiding principles
- Every new hire has a working laptop, the access needed for their role and a defined first-day schedule before they arrive.
- Access is granted on a least-privilege basis.
- Spending follows the budget caps in §3.2 unless an exception is approved under §9.
- Decisions that this handbook does not clearly answer are escalated to a person rather than guessed.

### How to use this handbook
- Read the section that matches the question: equipment in §3, access in §4, training in §5, remote work in §6, right-to-work in §7, the first day in §8 and exceptions in §9.
- When citing a rule in an onboarding plan, cite the most specific section id available, for example §3.2 rather than §3.
- Numeric limits in this handbook mirror the machine-readable policy tables maintained by People & Culture. If a reader finds two different numbers for the same rule, the case is handled under §9.1.
- The handbook is reviewed once a year, and interim changes are announced by email to all hiring managers.

### Definitions
- Start date: the first working day of the employee, as stated on the intake form.
- Level: seniority band of the hire, one of junior, medior, senior or lead.
- Catalog: the list of equipment items that IT can order, each with a catalog identifier, a price and a stock status.
- Case: a single onboarding request for a single new hire, from intake until completion.
- Escalation: handing a case to a named human decision maker instead of completing it automatically.

## §2 Roles and responsibilities

### HR Coordinator
- Owns the onboarding case from intake to completion.
- Verifies the intake form for completeness: name, personal email, role, level, start date, manager, location and contract type.
- Opens the right-to-work verification task when the hire requires a visa (see §7).
- Confirms that the day-1 schedule contains every mandatory event (see §8.1).
- Reviews escalated cases and routes them to the HR manager.

### IT Department
- Orders and configures equipment from the approved catalog.
- Provisions accounts for the systems listed in §4.1.
- Reports licence shortages (for example, a creative-software pool being exhausted) to HR within one working day, and proposes an alternative where one exists.
- Runs the IT set-up session on day 1.

### Hiring Manager
- Submits the intake form at least 10 calendar days before the start date.
- Confirms the role and level of the new hire.
- Holds a one-to-one meeting with the new hire on day 1.
- Nominates a buddy for the first weeks.
- Approves requested extras within the limits of §3.2.

### Employee
- Completes compliance and security training within the deadlines in §5.1.
- Supplies right-to-work documents on time when requested.
- Reports equipment or access problems to IT in the first week.
- Reads this handbook and the security guidelines shared on day 1.

### Service levels
- HR acknowledges a new intake form within one working day.
- IT confirms equipment availability within two working days of receiving the case.
- The hiring manager answers questions from HR or IT within two working days.
- Escalated cases are reviewed by the HR manager within three working days. Cases with a start date inside the next ten working days are reviewed first.

### Communication
- All onboarding communication about a case is logged on the case itself.
- New hires receive a welcome email with the schedule for the first week, the list of systems they will receive and the equipment that will be delivered.
- Hiring managers are copied on the welcome email and receive a short summary of open items.

## §3 Equipment policy

### §3.1 Standard equipment kits
Every hire receives a kit appropriate to their role. All items must come from the approved equipment catalog.

- Software engineer, engineering manager: laptop, one external monitor, keyboard, mouse, headset.
- Data analyst: laptop, one external monitor, keyboard, mouse, headset.
- Finance officer, HR coordinator, sales representative: laptop, keyboard, mouse, headset; monitor on request.
- Office-based employees also receive a dock at their assigned desk.
- Requested extras (for example a second monitor, an ergonomic chair or a webcam) are considered on top of the standard kit but count towards the budget cap in §3.2.
- Accessibility needs declared on the intake form take priority over the standard kit and are accommodated first.
- A laptop is always part of the kit. Employees get one laptop only; a replacement is ordered through IT support, not through onboarding.
- Choose the laptop that fits the role. Engineering and data roles may need a more powerful machine, but the total still has to fit the budget cap in §3.2.
- Where two items serve the same purpose, prefer the in-stock item with the lower catalog price.
- Peripherals such as keyboards, mice, headsets and webcams are part of the kit and count towards the budget.
- Furniture, such as an ergonomic chair or a desk, is only provided where a documented need exists, for example an accessibility need or a remote setup agreed with HR.
- Quantities above one for any item need a written reason on the case.

### §3.2 Budget caps per level
The total equipment budget per new hire, including all extras, is capped by seniority level. Totals are calculated at catalog price multiplied by quantity.

- Junior: €1,800 total.
- Medior: €2,200 total.
- Senior: €2,600 total.
- Lead: €3,000 total.

Rules for applying the caps:
- The cap applies to the sum of all equipment lines for the hire.
- Catalog prices are authoritative. A price quoted by a requester or supplier does not change the cost of an item.
- Items that are out of stock cannot be ordered and must be replaced by an in-stock alternative of similar function.
- Items that are not in the catalog cannot be ordered under this policy; request a catalog addition from IT.
- A plan that exceeds the cap must be revised, for example by choosing a lower-priced laptop, or must go through the exception route in §9.
- Quantities are multiplied by the unit price. Two monitors at €289 each count as €578.
- The cap is a ceiling per hire. Unused budget cannot be carried over to another employee or to a later order.
- VAT is not added on top; catalog prices are the amounts compared with the cap.
- Budget exceptions need written approval from the HR manager under §9.2 and are recorded on the case.

Worked example:
- A junior hire has a cap of €1,800.
- A laptop at €1,249 plus a monitor at €289 plus a keyboard at €89 totals €1,627, which is within the cap.
- Adding a €449 monitor brings the total to €2,076, which exceeds the cap, so the plan must be revised.

### §3.3 Ordering process
- IT places orders only after the case has passed policy review.
- Orders are placed against catalog identifiers, never against free-text descriptions.
- Delivery to the office or the remote address is targeted for at least two working days before the start date.
- If a delivery will miss the start date, IT informs the hiring manager and HR.
- Returned or replaced items are tracked by IT and do not count against the budget of the new hire.
- IT checks stock before an order is confirmed. If an item sells out between planning and ordering, IT proposes the closest in-stock alternative and the case is updated.
- Orders for several items are consolidated into a single delivery where possible.
- Every order is linked to the case identifier so that finance can trace it.
- IT keeps the serial numbers of delivered devices and registers them in the asset system.
- Standard kits are ordered once the plan has passed policy review. Extras are ordered after the hiring manager has confirmed them.

## §4 System access policy

### §4.1 Access matrix
Access is granted by role. A system that is not listed for a role is not provisioned automatically.

- Software engineer: email, slack, gitlab, vpn, jira, aws_dev.
- Data analyst: email, slack, jira, tableau, snowflake_read, vpn.
- Finance officer: email, slack, erp_finance, expense_tool.
- HR coordinator: email, slack, hris, ats.
- Sales representative: email, slack, crm, expense_tool.
- Engineering manager: email, slack, gitlab, vpn, jira, aws_dev, hris_team_view.

Additional notes:
- Access is requested by IT against the matrix; the hiring manager cannot add systems by email.
- Software engineers may also request the creative-software licence (adobe_cc) where a design task justifies it. Licences are limited.
- A requested system that is not in the matrix for the role is declined and the requester is told which systems are allowed.
- Email and slack are provisioned for every hire on or before the start date.
- Accounts are created in a proposed state and activated on the start date, never earlier.
- Every access request carries a short justification linking the system to the duties of the role.
- Access is reviewed again when the employee changes role or level.
- If a provisioning request fails, for example because a licence pool is exhausted, IT proposes an alternative and records a follow-up task for the original request.

### §4.2 Privileged systems
- Privileged systems are aws_prod, erp_admin and hris.
- Access to a privileged system by a role that the matrix does not allow always requires approval by the CISO.
- Such requests are never approved automatically; they are routed to a human reviewer.
- Privileged access is time-boxed and reviewed every quarter.
- A privileged system that the matrix does allow for the role, such as hris for an HR coordinator, follows the normal provisioning flow.
- A new hire never receives privileged access before they have completed security training.
- Requests need a business justification, the name of the requesting manager and an end date.
- The security team keeps a register of every privileged access decision.

### §4.3 Contractor restrictions
- Contractors receive access only to the systems needed for their assignment.
- Contractors never receive aws_dev.
- Contractors do not receive access to hris or erp systems of any kind.
- Contract end dates are recorded so that access can be removed on the last working day.
- Contractors use company equipment from the standard kit only where the contract says so; otherwise they bring their own device and receive a security-checked remote desktop.
- A contractor's hiring manager confirms the list of required systems in writing.
- Contractor accounts expire automatically at the contract end date unless extended by HR.

## §5 Training & compliance

### §5.1 Mandatory training timeline
- Compliance training must be scheduled within 14 days after the start date.
- Security training must be scheduled within 30 days after the start date for every employee whose role has vpn access or any aws_ system.
- Employees whose roles hold neither vpn nor aws access still complete compliance training but are not required to take the security training.
- A training is considered scheduled when it appears as a calendar event for the employee.
- Training that is scheduled after its deadline is treated as a policy deviation and must be corrected.
- Deadlines are counted in calendar days from the start date. Compliance training is therefore due by start date plus 14 days and security training by start date plus 30 days.
- Scheduling the training on the deadline day itself is allowed.
- Training should be placed in the first week where possible, because early completion reduces risk.
- Each training is scheduled as a separate calendar event with a clear title.
- Employees with a contractor agreement complete the same trainings as employees.

### §5.2 Delivery method
- Compliance training is delivered as an online course of about 60 minutes.
- Security training is a live session of about 90 minutes, run by the Information Security team.
- Employees who miss a session are rebooked within five working days.
- Completion is recorded by HR.
- Reminders are sent one week and one day before each session.
- Employees with accessibility needs receive materials in an accessible format on request.
- Repeat training is required each year; the first date is set by HR when the initial training is completed.

## §6 Remote work policy

### §6.1 Remote-eligible roles
- All roles in the access matrix are eligible for remote work within the Netherlands.
- Remote work is agreed in the contract or an addendum and recorded in the intake form location field.
- Remote employees must reside in the Netherlands for tax and social security reasons.
- Remote work from abroad is not covered by this handbook and is referred to HR under §9.
- Managers confirm that the role can be performed remotely before the intake form is submitted.
- Remote employees have the same working hours, reporting lines and performance expectations as office-based employees.

### §6.2 Remote setup standard
- A stable internet connection and a suitable workspace are the employee's responsibility.
- The company supplies the standard kit in §3.1.
- Remote employees join the day-1 schedule by video, and IT performs set-up remotely.
- Remote employees visit an office for an in-person welcome at least once in the first quarter.
- Remote employees receive a dedicated contact in IT for the first month.
- The company covers a standard home-office internet allowance as set out in the compensation policy, which is separate from this handbook.
- Security rules apply in the home office in the same way as in the office: device encryption, screen lock and use of the company VPN where the role has vpn access.

### §6.3 Remote equipment
- Equipment is delivered to the home address given on the intake form.
- Remote hires follow the same budget caps as office hires, subject to §6.4.
- Equipment remains company property and is returned at the end of the contract.
- Ergonomic furniture for remote workers is handled through the ordering process in §3.3.
- Remote employees are asked to confirm delivery within two working days, and IT follows up on missed deliveries.
- Technical problems with remote equipment are handled by IT support under the same service levels as in the office.

### §6.4 Remote monitor allowance
- Remote employees receive a home-office monitor allowance of €500.
- The allowance is used for external monitors only.
- The allowance is claimed through the equipment order and is applied when the employee's contract is signed.
- The allowance is available once per remote employee and is not repeated on renewal of the contract.
- Employees who change from remote to office work return any monitor bought under the allowance if they leave within the first year.

## §7 Visa & right-to-work

### §7.1 Documentation
- Every employee must prove their right to work in the Netherlands before they begin work.
- When the intake form states that a visa is required, HR opens a task titled "Right to work verification" owned by HR.
- Employees are asked for a passport, residence permit or visa, and any sponsor documents.
- Documents are stored in the HR system and retained according to the data retention policy.
- Only HR staff handle right-to-work documents. Hiring managers see only whether the check is complete.
- The task title must contain the words "right to work" so that it can be found in the case overview.
- Where a hire is a Dutch or EU citizen, HR still checks identity but no visa is required.
- Fixed-term and contractor hires follow the same process as permanent hires.

### §7.2 Timelines
- The right-to-work task must be created and assigned before the start date.
- Where the visa is not yet issued, the start date can only be confirmed by the HR manager.
- A hire without verified right-to-work documents cannot start work.
- Immigration procedures can take several weeks, so HR starts the task as soon as the intake form arrives.
- HR informs the hiring manager if the timeline puts the start date at risk.
- Changes to the start date are recorded on the case and the day-1 schedule is regenerated.

## §8 Day-1 schedule requirements

### §8.1 Mandatory day-1 events
Every new hire has the following events on the start date:
- IT set-up session (event type it_setup).
- One-to-one meeting with the hiring manager (event type manager_1on1).
- HR introduction (event type hr_intro).

All three events must take place on the start date itself, not on a later day.

### §8.2 Recommended day-1 to day-5 events
- Buddy lunch in the first week (event type buddy_lunch).
- Team introduction meeting.
- Walkthrough of the tools used by the team.
- Compliance and security trainings booked within the deadlines in §5.1.
- Meeting with the buddy to discuss tools, rituals and where to find help.
- Check-in with HR at the end of the first week.

### §8.3 Practical rules
- Day 1 begins with the IT set-up session so that the new hire has working access before other meetings.
- Typical durations are 30 to 60 minutes per event, and events must not overlap.
- If the start date falls on a public holiday, the hiring manager and HR agree a new start date, and the case goes through §9.1 if the notice period is affected.
- Remote hires receive video links for every event in the welcome email.
- Events are created as calendar entries for the new hire and the hiring manager once the case is approved.

## §9 Escalation & exceptions

### §9.1 When escalation is required
- Any request that this handbook does not clearly cover.
- Any request for privileged systems that the matrix does not allow (§4.2).
- A start date with less than 10 calendar days of notice.
- A manager who cannot be found in the HR system.
- A hire whose email address already exists in the HR system.
- Any case where two policy statements appear to conflict.
- Any plan that remains non-compliant after the automated corrections are exhausted.
- Any request where the available policy guidance is unclear or the retrieved guidance does not match the question.
- Any request for equipment or access that falls outside the standard kit, the catalog or the matrix and that the requester considers essential.

Examples of non-escalation:
- A plan that is over budget because of a single laptop choice is corrected automatically by choosing a cheaper laptop.
- An out-of-stock item is replaced by an in-stock alternative.
- A missing training event is added to the schedule.

### §9.2 Approval authority
- The HR manager decides on escalated onboarding cases.
- The CISO decides on privileged access.
- The hiring manager cannot approve exceptions on their own.
- The HR manager may approve, reject or send a case back with a comment.
- An approval of an escalated case lets the onboarding complete as planned. A rejection stops the case and informs the hiring manager.
- If the HR manager is absent, the deputy HR manager has the same authority.

### §9.3 Documentation
- The reason for escalation is recorded on the case.
- The decision, the approver and a short comment are recorded on the case.
- Approved exceptions are reported to People & Culture each quarter.
- Rejected cases are kept on file for the same period as approved ones.
- The audit trail of each case lists the steps taken, the checks performed and the policy sections cited.
- Personal data in case records is limited to what is needed for onboarding and is deleted according to the data retention policy.
